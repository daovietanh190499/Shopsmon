How many trial runs are enough to trust an auto-approver? Zero failures is not zero risk

After 0 failures in n cases, the 95 % upper bound on the wrong-approval rate is only about 3/n: certifying 1 % takes 299 labelled cases and 0.1 % takes 2,995. The Wald bound covers only 63.40 % at p = 1 %, n = 100, and looking after every case issues a wrong certificate 21.73 % of the time instead of at most 5 %; dividing alpha over 20 looks holds it at 1.09 %.

A Kalman filter for robot position: how much measuring really gains, and when an outlier gate locks itself out

In a fixed-seed one-dimensional simulation, a matched Kalman filter cuts position RMSE by 43.6 % against raw fixes and 36.8 % against the best EWMA, and velocity RMSE by 8.8 times against differencing two fixes; mis-specifying the noise by ten times raises position RMSE by 15 % to 35 % and skews NEES by up to 10 times; a 99 % outlier gate got stuck in 4 to 38 of 1,000 runs, a reset rule in none.

Send-on-delta: how many sensor messages it saves and what it costs in error

A deadband of one sigma still sends 48 % of samples on a constant signal; on a noiseless random walk send-on-delta cuts RMSE by 42 % to 46 % against periodic sampling at the same message count and never exceeds its threshold; on a noisy signal, thresholding raw samples is worse than periodic raw sending, while thresholding an EWMA beats the block mean by 10 % to 38 %.
