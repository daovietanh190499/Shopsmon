Android Authority tests 2026 phone chips: Apple A20 Pro is 37% ahead in single-core over the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6, with Tensor G6 trailing in the CPU and GPU tests

2026-11-11 · Source: Android Authority

According to Robert Triggs' test on Android Authority (Nov 11, 2026), four top 2026 phone chips were compared on four real handsets: the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 in the REDMAGIC 12 Pro, Google's Tensor G6 in the Pixel 11 Pro XL, MediaTek's Dimensity 9600 Pro in the OPPO Find X10 Pro Max and Apple's A20 Pro in the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Samsung's Exynos 2700 has not been unveiled (it is expected with the Galaxy S27 series) and was not tested. On specifications, the Snapdragon, Dimensity and A20 Pro are all built on TSMC's 2 nm process while Tensor G6 uses 3 nm; the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 has two Prime cores at up to 5.0 GHz and six Performance cores at up to 4.0 GHz (Oryon architecture), the Dimensity 9600 Pro has a 2+3+3 CPU layout with two C2-Ultra cores at up to 4.55 GHz, and Tensor G6 uses older Arm C1 cores. On the CPU, according to Geekbench 7, the Snapdragon is roughly 12% faster than the Dimensity 9600 Pro in single-core and 14% in multi-core, and 29% faster than Tensor G6 in single-core and 59% in multi-core. The A20 Pro in the iPhone 18 Pro Max scores 37% higher in single-core and just under 6% higher in multi-core than the Snapdragon-powered phone. On graphics, Qualcomm's Adreno GPU leads the A20 Pro's GPU by 6% in Wild Life Extreme and 8% in the Solar Bay ray-tracing test and beats the Dimensity 9600 Pro by 20% in ray tracing; in rasterisation Apple and MediaTek are virtually level; all three chips are more than twice as fast as Tensor G6, which does not support ray tracing. In sustained stress tests, the REDMAGIC 12 Pro has liquid cooling and a fan that let internal temperatures reach 58°C and make its exterior very hot, while the other phones generally stay below 40–45°C; the Dimensity 9600 Pro initially delivers performance close to Apple's but falls behind as it heats up. The author cautions that results can vary considerably with each handset's cooling and board layout. The article also notes that Tensor G6 supports SME2 features for AI workloads and uses Google's custom TPU, while all three rivals have invested heavily in dedicated NPUs.
