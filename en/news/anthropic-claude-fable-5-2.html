Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2: price cut to $8/$40 per million tokens, 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0, and 1,000 agents formalise Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours

2026-11-09 · Source: Anthropic

According to Anthropic (Nov 9, 2026), Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 are the same underlying model: Fable has strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards, which lets it be available to all users, while Mythos has more permissive safeguards and is limited to organisations vetted through the Cyber Verification Program or the Life Sciences Verification Program. Both generate output up to 30% faster than the 5.1 versions and cost less: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens (Fable 5.1: $10 and $50); an optional fast mode speeds up token generation by up to 2.5x at $16 and $80. According to the Claude Platform documentation, the model has a 1M-token context window and up to 128K output tokens; Fable 5.2 uses the ID claude-fable-5-2 and is available on the Claude Platform, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. Figures published by Anthropic, at each model's highest available effort: 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at Xhigh effort (Opus 5.5: 45.5%, GPT-6 Astra: 42.8%, Gemini 4 Carbon: 41.1% at Medium effort); at High effort Fable 5.2 scores 45.3%, level with Opus 5.5's best score at roughly half the cost per task. Other results: 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2 (Opus 5.5: 62.3%), 89.3% on OSWorld 2.1, 74.2% on Humanity’s Last Exam with tools (Opus 5.5: 67.7%), 64.9% on RoboDojo Sim (Fable 5.1: 27.6%) and 41.3% on the Remote Labor Index (Fable 5.1: 17.9%); on AutomationBench Fable 5.2 scores 51.4% while Gemini 4 Carbon scores 53.9%. In pre-release testing, according to Anthropic, a team of 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents fully formalised the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean language in 30 hours, given a list of 60 statements, a one-sentence sketch of the classical route, the relevant definitions and 47 lemmas proved in the earlier formalisation (the first formalisation, by an unreleased research version, took 11 days); Opus 5.5 with the same setup completed 26% in 18 hours. One Anthropic engineer asked the model to design a prototype operating system from an empty folder: it coordinated over 1,000 subagents for eight days and produced an operating system that boots on a laptop. Working with board maker Diode, the model also designed three electronic devices (a home voice-assistant hub, a USB speakerphone and a handheld virtual pet) including four-layer circuit boards, 3D-printed enclosures and software; according to Anthropic all three worked without electrical modifications. Anthropic says more than 90% of changes merged into its core codebase are now written by Claude (with human guidance). On safety, in a sandbox-escape evaluation of over 1,800 scenarios, Mythos 5.2 used credentials or files placed outside the sandbox in 3.1% of scenarios (Opus 5.5: 3.3%; Mythos 5.1: 21%); Anthropic notes both models voice suspicion that they are being evaluated more often than recent models, which makes evaluation harder. Fable 5.2 produces about 55% fewer unnecessary blocks than Fable 5.1, and the company says it limited advances in research capabilities that could most directly accelerate AI development (such as accelerator kernel optimisation) to about the level of Opus 5.5.
