Infineon introduces AURIX RC1: its first RISC-V automotive MCU platform, with up to 12 32-bit cores and 40 MB of embedded memory

2026-11-04 · Source: Infineon

According to Infineon's announcement (Nov 4, 2026), AURIX RC1 is the company's first microcontroller platform based on the open RISC-V instruction set architecture, within the AURIX series, for software-defined vehicles (SDV), other software-defined applications (SDX) and Physical AI. The platform merges the strengths of AURIX and the TRAVEO T2G family (body, connectivity, display) into one joint MCU generation, spanning zone control, body, chassis, battery management and powertrain through to driver-assistance safety management and cockpit. Depending on the application, AURIX RC1 integrates up to 12 32-bit processing cores, a parallel processing unit for edge AI and up to 40 MB of embedded memory; Infineon also plans a product lineup with additional 64-bit microprocessor capabilities. The platform is designed to support applications up to the highest automotive safety level (ASIL D) and has cybersecurity mechanisms meeting ISO/SAE 21434 and the Cyber Resilience Act, hardware physical protection, crypto-agility, and readiness for post-quantum cryptography. For in-vehicle networking the family supports CAN-XL, 10BASE-T1S and multi-Gigabit Ethernet; selected devices include BRIGHTLANE Ethernet switching technology and hardware-accelerated data routing. The DRIVECORE software ecosystem combines pre-integrated bundles from Infineon and partners (AUTOSAR, open-source software, functional safety, cybersecurity), with virtual prototyping to start development before hardware is available and AI-assisted migration tools for porting software. Infineon will present the AURIX RC1 family at electronica (Munich, Nov 10–13, Hall C3, booth 502) and aims to provide samples in the first half of 2027.
