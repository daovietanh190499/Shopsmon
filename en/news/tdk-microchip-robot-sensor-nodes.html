TDK and Microchip show a distributed sensor-node platform for robots at electronica 2026: dsPIC33A, a 6-axis IMU and 10BASE-T1S single-pair Ethernet

2026-11-10 · Source: TDK / InvenSense

According to TDK's announcement (Nov 10, 2026), TDK and Microchip Technology are demonstrating at electronica (Munich, TDK booth B5.520, Nov 10–13, 2026) a distributed sensor-node platform for next-generation robots. The platform combines sensing technology from InvenSense (a TDK group company) with Microchip’s dsPIC33A digital signal controller (DSC) and 10BASE-T1S single-pair Ethernet connectivity based on the LAN8651/LAN8679, for real-time processing and edge AI in robotics. According to the announcement, humanoid robots may carry 100 or more sensors for perception, motor control, force and torque measurement, joint monitoring, safety and environmental awareness; the platform is designed to let developers integrate more intelligent sensor nodes throughout a robot while simplifying cabling and communications, and it processes sensor data locally on the dsPIC33A to reduce latency, network traffic and the compute load on the robot’s central controller. The demonstration board combines a dsPIC33A, 10BASE-T1S connectivity with the LAN8651/LAN8679, an InvenSense SmartMotion ICM-42688-P 6-axis IMU (a 3-axis accelerometer and a 3-axis gyroscope) and a MikroE expansion connector for other compatible TDK sensors, such as the recently launched PositionSense 9-axis solution for absolute orientation detection. According to the announcement, the dsPIC33A combines a 200 MHz 32-bit core, DSP acceleration and a double-precision floating-point unit with high-speed ADCs and high-resolution PWM; each node can acquire data, condition signals, filter, fuse sensors, run diagnostics and execute machine learning before sending relevant information to the central controller. In the demonstration the dsPIC33A runs a machine-learning algorithm for real-time gesture recognition from several sensors. Beyond robotics, the announcement names predictive maintenance (detecting vibration, shock, tilt and rotation in motors, pumps, gearboxes, compressors and fans) and distributed monitoring of industrial equipment (production machinery, conveyors, HVAC systems, energy equipment). TDK is also showing TMR and Hall sensors for precise angle detection, MEMS microphones for acoustic activity detection, temperature sensors, pressure sensors for tactile sensing and 9-axis IMU+TMR sensing. According to the announcement, the building blocks of the demonstration board are already available.
