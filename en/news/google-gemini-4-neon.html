Google introduces Gemini 4 Neon: $3/$15 per million tokens, a 1M-token context, #3 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and #1 on its Coding Agent Index

2026-11-18 · Source: Google

According to Google (Nov 18, 2026, a post by Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP of Google DeepMind and Chief AI Architect), Gemini 4 Neon is a new model in the Gemini 4 family aimed at complex software engineering, knowledge work and science workflows; within the family, Carbon handles the hardest long-horizon projects, Hydrogen handles fast everyday execution, and Neon balances depth and efficiency. It is priced at $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 per million cached input tokens, a 95% discount) and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window; according to the Google AI Studio documentation, the model ID is gemini-4-neon with four thinking levels: low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh. According to Google, at xhigh Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (#3 overall, just behind larger, slower models), ranks #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%) and #2 with 1336 on the new Agent Arena Index (more than 4,200 real-world agent sessions). For agentic coding, Google cites #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index (58.6), 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 (at the default high setting) and 39.0% on SWE-sweep when run inside Google Antigravity, versus 12.8% with the mini-SWE harness. For enterprise work, Google says Neon ranks first on Zapier’s AutomationBench (56.3%), Andon Labs’ Vending-Bench 2 ($16,843), the Legal Research Bench and the Tax Agent Bench, and scores 75.8% on Agents’ Last Exam v1.1. On cost, at high Neon has a 32% lower cost per task than Carbon, and according to Google roughly 50% to 80% lower than top competing frontier models on third-party benchmarks. On cybersecurity, Google cites 90.5% on SEC-bench Pro, 51.26% on WebExploitBench v1.2 and 70.7% on Collinear’s CWE-bench v2; a Neon for cyber version will reach trusted defenders in the Fairwind Program over the next few days. On safety, Google reports a 0.09% misaligned-action rate on its agentic safety suite, and says Neon resisted every attack in Gray Swan’s indirect prompt injection benchmark when real-time safeguards were enabled at its highest thinking level. Neon began rolling out on Nov 18 in the Gemini API (paid tier) in Google AI Studio and Android Studio, Gemini for business (via the API and model dropdown), Google Antigravity (for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers) and the Gemini app (Pro and Ultra). These figures are published by Google and cite results from third-party evaluators and internal evaluations.
