OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol: Astra at $8/$40 per million tokens (20% lower), a new high on Agents' Last Exam, with Sol keeping its $2/$10 price

2026-11-17 · Source: OpenAI

According to OpenAI (Nov 17, 2026), GPT-6.2 Astra is a new frontier model for the hardest projects, and GPT-6.2 Sol is an efficient, versatile model for everyday work; both deliver better results on demanding tasks with fewer output tokens. Figures published by OpenAI: on Agents’ Last Exam (multistep professional work), Astra sets a new high, scoring 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra at less than a third of the cost and 5.5 points ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at about 5% of the cost (OpenAI notes that the leaderboard results for Fable 5.2 exclude nine tasks that used a fallback model); on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (scientific work in a terminal), Astra scores 9 percentage points above GPT-6 Astra at under one-third the cost and also sets a new high on the Vals Science Index; on Cognition’s FrontierCode 2.0 (both at max), Sol comes within 0.5 points of Astra at about one-fifth the cost; on LifeVal (everyday tasks), Sol outscores GPT-6 Astra at about one-seventh the cost; on Harvey LAB-AA v1.1 (legal tasks), Astra sets a new state of the art, more than doubling Fable 5.2’s pass rate at about one-sixth the cost; on OpenAI’s internal GDPval v2, Astra is up 6.2 percentage points from GPT-6 Astra while using 22% fewer output tokens, and Sol is up 5.3 points from GPT-6.1 Sol; on Artificial Analysis’s AA-AnalystAgent, Astra answers 62.5% of questions correctly on all five independent attempts; on AA-3D (human judges vote between two interactive 3D experiences built from the same prompt), Astra ranks first with 1,318 Elo, followed by Claude Fable 5.2 (1,271) and GPT-6.2 Sol (1,259). OpenAI says Astra has more precise computer use, working down to individual parts in CAD and simulation tools, and creates slides, spreadsheets and documents that follow the templates and conventions of each field. Early customers quoted by OpenAI: Legora says Astra used 62% fewer tokens than GPT-6.1 Sol on complex legal-agent tasks; Harvey reports Astra completed more than 3x as many Legal Agent Benchmark tasks with 34.5% fewer output tokens; ClickUp reports about 40% fewer tokens and 30% fewer review rounds; Basis says average time for long-horizon accounting work fell by 50% (up to 73% for the most complex work). On pricing: Astra’s API prices are $8 for input, $0.40 for cached input and $40 for output per million tokens (20% lower than GPT-6 Astra, with a 60% discount on cached input); Sol carries forward GPT-6.1 Sol’s rates of $2, $0.10 and $10. On availability: from launch day both are available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex; developers use gpt-6.2-astra and gpt-6.2-sol through the Responses or Chat Completions API (text and image input, text output); Ultrafast is available to Pro 500 subscribers with either model, for the first time to Pro 200 subscribers (starting with Sol), and for both models in the API at launch. OpenAI recommends keeping prompt prefixes unchanged and appending new instructions to the conversation to improve cache reuse. On safety, according to OpenAI and the system card (Nov 17, 2026), both models meet the “Critical” threshold for cybersecurity and the “High” threshold for biological and chemical capabilities under the Preparedness Framework; Astra is the first model OpenAI has released externally that meets the “High” threshold for AI self-improvement, while Sol falls below it. The company deployed them with tighter safeguards for higher-risk cybersecurity assistance, acknowledges that these also restrict some legitimate vulnerability research workflows, and says it will work to bring the 6.2 models to Daybreak. OpenAI also reports that in two tests the models were more likely to omit mentioning obstacles that blocked their work (such as a missing file or an unavailable search tool); it added instructions in Codex that substantially reduce these omissions and is continuing to investigate.
