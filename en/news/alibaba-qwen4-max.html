Alibaba releases Qwen4-Max: a 2.4-trillion-parameter MoE with 71 billion active parameters per token, a 1M-token context and about 60% lower cost per task

2026-11-16 · Source: Alibaba Cloud

According to an Alibaba Cloud announcement (Hangzhou, Nov 16, 2026), Qwen4-Max is the most capable model in the Qwen family to date, with 2.4 trillion total parameters, native vision capabilities and a one-million-token context window. The Qwen4 architecture upgrades the sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design across attention, residual connections and embeddings; the model activates only 71 billion parameters per token, 25% fewer than Qwen3.8-Max at the same 2.4-trillion scale. According to heise online (Nov 16), Qwen3.8-Max activates 95 billion parameters, Qwen4-Max adds 328 billion parameters for N-gram embeddings (a lookup table of short token-sequence patterns), accepts text, images and video but outputs text only; the model had a preview on Nov 11 and is now generally available via API. It is a proprietary model without open weights; Alibaba says smaller open-weight models from the Qwen4 series will be released soon. On performance, Alibaba states that Qwen4-Max ranked 6th on Code Arena (a real-world coding leaderboard) and posted strong scores on benchmarks for agent workflows, workplace productivity and visual reasoning, including Agents’ Last Exam, Vals Index, JobBench and Chartography. According to Work360 — Alibaba’s proprietary benchmark across 721 specialised fields — Qwen4-Max outperforms Qwen3.8-Max overall while using 35% fewer tokens at High reasoning effort and reducing the average cost per task by around 60%. Examples published by Alibaba: autonomous optimisation of cloud resource-scheduling workflows (an average 9% cut in data-annotation model call costs and offline CPU rescheduling more than 14 times faster); in automotive manufacturing, a 95% cut in manual workpiece programming time, from roughly 475 minutes to 24 minutes, by catching overcuts and insufficient material removal before machining; a continuous 48-hour agent run through four iterations of a closed loop (molecular design, synthesis and testing, active learning) that found an experimentally validated chip coolant molecule; and, in an Alzheimer’s research experiment, 454 tool calls and 3,239 metric evaluations to design and optimise 24 candidate molecules. On pricing, according to heise citing Alibaba’s official price list: $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens (25% less than its predecessor), with cached input at $0.075 per million tokens. The model is available on QwenCloud (via Token Plan and API), QwenWork and QwenStudio, and according to heise also through OpenRouter and Vercel AI Gateway. On independent evaluation, according to heise citing Artificial Analysis, Qwen4-Max scores 43 on the Intelligence Index at its highest reasoning level, nine points above Qwen3.8-Max, while Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 46, Gemini 4 Carbon 60 and Claude Fable 5.2 leads with 65; on Terminal-Bench 4.0 Kimi scores 67% versus 48% for Qwen4-Max; on the Vals Index Qwen4-Max scores 63.20% versus 62.52% for Kimi. Artificial Analysis calculates a cost of $3.27 per Intelligence Index task, versus $10.02 for Qwen3.8-Max and $3.68 for Kimi K3.1 Pro; Qwen4-Max generates about 105,000 output tokens per task, 39% fewer than its predecessor but more than Kimi (about 72,000). According to TheEdge (Nov 19), at Qwen Conference Malaysia 2026 an Alibaba Cloud representative said the model can take longer on some tasks because it performs additional checks on its output, and the company announced a third data centre in Johor, bringing its total in Malaysia to six.
